Usage Rules — Where Each Color Lives

Surface
Use
Avoid
Navy
Primary brand surface. Yard signs, truck doors, social headers, polos, footers, hero blocks. The default place the badge lives.
Don't use as a body text color on cream — it works, but Ink (#15161A) reads cleaner for long copy.
Brick
Emphasis only — call-to-action buttons, key numerals, the "PROS" half of split lockups, storm/insurance messaging.
Never as a large background block. Brick is a stamp, not a wall.
Brass
Trim and structure — rules, dashes, borders, picket motifs, "premium" callouts, the tagline in dark contexts.
Don't set body copy in brass on cream. Brass is a finish, not a paint.
Cream
The "paper" surface for documents, light marketing, and the upper field of the badge. Warmer than white, on purpose.
Don't substitute pure white where cream is called for — it cools the system and breaks the heritage feel.

Oswald is the spiritual sibling of the FENCEPROS wordmark — a condensed grotesque with broad shoulders. Use it for headlines, eyebrows, decorative all-caps, and any signage that needs to read from a curb. Always tracked open +10 to +120 in caps; tight at display sizes.

Hierarchy Rules

  • One Display per layout, max.
  • Headlines in Oswald. Body in Source Sans 3. Never the reverse.
  • All-caps tracking ≥ +60 below 24px.

Color & Type

  • Display in Navy, Brick, or Cream — never Brass for readable type.
  • Body in Ink (#15161A) or Char (#2C2F36).
  • Use Brick on Cream for the single most important phrase per page.

Things Not To Do

  • No italics for emphasis. Use weight or color.
  • No serif fonts anywhere except email signature (legacy mail clients).
  • No condensing Source Sans. No stretching Oswald.
04Logo Usage

How The Badge
Travels.

The badge is sturdy but not invincible. Give it room. Place it on the right surface. Choose the right lockup for the moment. When in doubt, use the primary full-color badge on cream with breathing room.

Clear Space

The minimum clear space around the badge equals ½ × the height of the star roundel. Nothing — type, edges, photographs, other marks — may enter that zone.

Horizontal Lockup

The badge is tall. For website headers, email signatures, document letterheads, and narrow horizontal placements, use the horizontal lockup: badge on the left, set wordmark on the right, separated by a brass keyline. The wordmark uses Oswald 700, all caps, tracked +5.

FENCEPROS

Lockup minimum height: 48 px digital, 0.4 in print. Keyline thickness scales with badge height.

Reduction Mark — Favicon, App Icon, Stitch

At sizes below the badge minimum (favicons, app icons, breast embroidery, social profile pictures), use the Star Coin — the roundel from the badge, set on navy. It carries the brand at any size, down to 16 px.

The Star Coin
Navy field, brick roundel, brass ring, cream star. The inverted version (right) is for cream-on-dark situations.

What Not To Do

Six ways to wreck the badge. Don't.

01 · Don't recolor
Navy, brick, brass, cream — those four. No teal experiments.
02 · Don't stretch
Always lock aspect ratio. The shield is a shield, not a flag.
03 · Don't rotate or tilt
The badge stands plumb. No jaunty angles, ever.
04 · Don't add effects
No drop-glows, bevels, embosses, neon, or gradients on the mark itself.
05 · Don't place on busy backgrounds
If the background competes, use a cream passport behind the badge.
06 · Don't redraw or outline
Use the supplied master files only. Don't trace it. Don't simplify it.
05Voice & Messaging

Calm Authority.
No Shouting.

FencePros sounds like the contractor a discerning homeowner is relieved to find: confident without bravado, warm without folksiness, technical without jargon. We finish sentences the way we finish fences — square, on time, and standing for years.

Brand Voice

We speak the way a fourth-generation Tulsa craftsman speaks to a neighbor who just lost a fence in last week's storm: matter-of-fact, fair, and quick to act. We tell the truth about timelines and materials. We don't oversell, but we never undersell our work either — because a FencePros fence is meant to outlast the homeowner who bought it. Heritage tone, modern delivery. Confidence that doesn't need to raise its voice.

07Photography & Imagery

Real Tulsa.
Real Hands.

Every photograph in the brand should look like it was taken on an actual Tulsa job — warm afternoon light, real homes, real crew, real cedar. We never use stock photography or AI-generated imagery for fences or job sites. Period.

The Three Shot Types

Type 01
Hero — The Finished Run
Type 02
Craft — Hands & Materials
Type 03
Place — The Tulsa Home

Placeholders shown — these will be replaced with commissioned photography from each install. Photographer brief: golden hour, 35mm or 50mm equivalent, no flash, no HDR.

Photograph This

  • Real Tulsa homes — brick, stone, board-and-batten exteriors.
  • Warm, natural late-afternoon light. Long shadows are fine.
  • Cedar grain. Concrete pours. Plumb bobs. Levels.
  • The crew at work, in branded polos — backs and hands, not staged smiles.
  • Storm damage / before-and-after pairs (these earn trust faster than any tagline).
  • Picket close-ups with brass hardware catching light.

Never Use

  • Stock photography of any kind — generic suburban lawns, smiling stock contractors, perfect blue skies.
  • AI-generated fences, homes, or crew.
  • Aggressive HDR, oversaturated grass, plastic-looking skies.
  • Drone shots that don't show a recognizable Tulsa-area neighborhood.
  • Photos of fences that aren't ours, even as "inspiration."
  • Vertical phone snaps for hero placements — book the photographer.
